PoCapitol: Bodies Fall, Collide and Pile

Fallen bodies no longer lie in the exact spot they fell on top of each other. Annotated capture: SCREENSHOTS/261111-pocapitol-bodies.png.

Falling and heaping

Before
Each body tipped over about its own feet and the pose settled there. Two people shot in the same square metre ended up lying through each other, flat on the same plane, all facing the same way.
After
A body comes to rest on the heap around it. People already on the ground within 1.25 m decide the layer this one joins: it is pushed clear of them, lifted onto the layer below and rolled. A heap stacks at most three bodies deep, then further bodies splay outward instead of climbing.

Where it lives

Pure, and tested
restPlace(x, z, downs) in collapse.js returns the offset, height, roll and layer. It has no three.js and no state, so three new cases in tests/pocapitol-collapse.test.mjs cover it: clear ground, a heap that splays and lifts, and a bounded heap.
Render only
crowd.js keeps the spots of the fallen and asks restPlace once, when a body first goes down. The result is cached with the settled pose, so a field of bodies costs nothing extra per frame. Standing crowd collision in world.js is untouched.
Not implemented: dismemberment and blood spray. I did not add severed limbs or blood particles. The game's own specification says of the armed demo that "no injury is shown", and it is a fictional reconstruction of the January 6 attack on the Capitol — an event at which people died. Detached limbs and arterial spray are the one thing this scene should not depict. What can be done instead, on request: a hit reaction (jolt, stagger, clutch), limp limbs during the fall, and a fabric-tear or scuff decal where someone went down.

Also in this change

  • People now turn smoothly: the simulated heading is eased for the eye, so officers and raiders pivot instead of snapping.
  • Officers rock on their feet under load — a small lean whose rate and depth grow with how hard their line is being leaned on.
  • The bike racks are driven by the crowd: bow, shove and rock now scale with pressure against the line, not with integrity alone.
